Design System

The design language of a home infant-allergy protocol app. A quiet, dense dark interface — Inter, an 8px grid, a single blurple accent used as line and glow — carrying a three-voice health-status palette where colour tells a parent to carry on, watch closely, or call 911.

02

Health-status semantics

The layer that makes this a medical companion, not a to-do list.

Three voices, each a first-class role — never the accent, never each other. A parent reads the colour before the words. Every screen speaks them the same way: a filled danger button, a tinted danger surface with a hairline edge, light-step text on that surface.

Safe · tolerated

Success

Dose taken, watch window clear, no reaction. Sage green — reassuring without shouting.

Mild · watch

Caution

Mild reaction, review pending, watch-window running. Amber — attention, not alarm.

Severe · emergency

Danger

Reaction plan, 911, severe symptoms. Coral red — the loudest voice, reserved for it.

07

Do & don't

Do

  • Reserve each semantic voice for its meaning — danger is the emergency, never a highlight.
  • Outline primary actions; let the accent live in lines, focus rings and glow.
  • Lean on the ramps for surfaces, borders and muted text — value carries hierarchy.
  • Keep the compact 0.70× spacing; this interface is dense on purpose.

Don't

  • Flood large areas with the accent or any saturated fill.
  • Use pure black or white, or bolden headings past 500.
  • Style a status with the accent, or borrow one semantic's colour for another.
  • Stack heavy shadows; elevation here is a hairline edge plus ambient darkness.
